Read a gene, and read the verdict from the engine that owns it

Three things, and the second is why the first was worth doing.

The genetics lane comes into the crate: `vox rna <sequence>` reads from the
first AUG, stops at a stop codon, and emits a glyph where the codon names one
of the twelve promoted amino acids. The table is parsed out of the Lean that
proves it by the generator that already wrote the Python one, so it now emits
`src/genetic_table.rs` as well and neither language can drift from the source.

Checking Rust against the Python lane on the same sequences turned up a word
the two disagreed on, and the disagreement was not in the lift. The crate had
its own hand-written verdict rule: linear pairing, T for any fork that closed,
B only for a fork open across a terminal. The SIXTEEN_3 engine reads it
otherwise. Pairing is CYCLIC, because a word is a loop and ROTAT is the cyclic
shift, so a rotation cutting through a region must not change the readout. T
requires WORK inside the paired region, since a split and fuse with nothing
between them is μ∘δ=id and verifies nothing. B is a dangling split anywhere,
not only at a terminal. There is one Grammar; the crate now reads that one, and
agrees with the engine on four hundred words.

That fix exposed the third. The linear-sweep lane built its words without the
merge pass, so it never emitted a single ∋ — every fork read as dangling and
the lane could not return T at all. Its B count was an artefact of its own
lifter. It goes through `recompile_function` now, like the descent lane, and
returns T where a fork really closes.

The 32-bit sweep still reports more open forks than descent does on the same
source, because splitting at every terminal cuts functions that have interior
returns. That is a property of the sweep, and the count is a triage surface,
not a defect count.
