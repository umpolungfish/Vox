Vox compiles and debugs proteins, not only genes

The genetic lane read a coding sequence — codons to the twelve marks, one glyph per
promoted amino acid. A protein is that sequence one layer up, the codon degeneracy
already collapsed, so it lifts more directly. Vox already carried the bijection: the
Lean-derived AA_GLYPH, twelve promoted amino acids on the twelve axes, agreeing with
red-hot_rebis's own AA→primitive map on the promoted set (Met Trp Cys Tyr Phe Ile
Asn Gln His Asp Lys Glu). What was missing was the door.

Three verbs, all lifting to the same word the rna lane produces and verdicting the
same closure law:

  vox aa <SEQ>       one-letter residues → word
  vox fasta <FILE>   protein from FASTA (headers dropped)
  vox pdb <FILE>     protein from a PDB, one residue per CA so an all-atom file
                     yields the sequence and not one glyph per atom

The eight ground-layer amino acids (A G P T S R L V) are silent, exactly as their
codons are in a transcript — a fact of the code, not a gap in the lift. A
three-letter table backs the PDB reader.

Compiling is the lift; debugging is the instruments on the result. rebis_chain_protein.pdb
lifts 61 promoted residues to a fold word that verdicts F — more ∋ than ∈, an
over-fused fold, which banked and insert then qualify (the weight survives its live
clears; nothing is exposed). Insulin's A-chain lifts to a T-closing word. The fold
is now a word, and every word instrument — rotat, weight, banked, trans, frames —
reads it.

Caution for the test data: red-hot_rebis/fasta/Insulin.fasta is a NUCLEOTIDE
sequence despite the name, so it belongs to `vox rna`, not `vox aa`; handed to the
protein lane its bare AUGC read as residues and only C promoted.
