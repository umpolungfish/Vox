vox glyco: locate the boundary interfaces on a peptide

A glycopeptide is a boundary interface in the corpus's own sense — the peptide is
the bulk, the glycan is the environment-facing boundary, and the sequon is the seam
between them. Vox read the bulk (vox aa/fasta/pdb) and nothing of the interface.

vox glyco names the interfaces, which is the half that reads off the peptide with
no new grounding. N-linked sequons are the determinate case: Asn-X-[Ser/Thr] with
X≠Pro, and Asn is ∈ — the recognition gate — so an N-site is an ∈ in the peptide
word that opens a sequon, reported with its position and motif. O-linked candidates
(Ser/Thr) are admitted but flagged as what they are: not determinate, and
GROUND-LAYER, so they carry no mark in the word and are invisible to a reading of
the backbone alone. That invisibility is a fact about the code worth stating, not a
gap.

Held back deliberately, and named in the output rather than faked: the glycan TREE
at each site is a branched word of its own, and lifting it needs the
monosaccharides grounded — GlcNAc, Man, Gal, Fuc, sialic acid — which is a job for
the chem pipeline (imas/fg_exhaustive already maps functional groups to opcodes),
not a sugar→mark table stipulated by hand. Hand-imscribing that table would be the
law_never_hand_imscribe violation; it goes through the pipeline next.
