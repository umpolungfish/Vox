The organism reads itself, and learns what B costs

`vox self` points the lifter at its own image. It comes back in phase: every
function by descent, F zero, which is the only signal that says the decoder and
the bytes agree.

What the self-image taught is worth more than the fact that it worked. Six
hundred of its own functions verdict B, which looked like either a defect in the
lifter or a truth about the code, and was neither. An early return IS a fork that
leaves and never rejoins, so a function with several exits carries several open
forks by construction. Measured across its own image the surplus of splits over
fuses rises with exits — 0.28 at none, 5.26 at one, 12.91 at two, 15.00 at four
or more — and above two exits essentially every function carries surplus.

Two hypotheses were tested and dropped before that one. Instruction order is not
the cause; the walker holds a function's body in an address-keyed map and the
emitted module has no descending step. Panic handlers are not the cause either:
functions that call one carry surplus at 38 percent against 39 percent for
functions that call none, which is no signal at all.

So ranking B candidates by raw surplus ranks them by how many ways a function can
return. `open_forks` now separates the surplus into the part the exits explain
and the residual that they do not, and `vox self` ranks by the residual. On its
own image the deepest residual belongs to the descent walker itself.

The README states the four words of the name and what each one names that can be
run, rather than leaving them as decoration.
