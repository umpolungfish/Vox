Execute 32-bit x86, and fix the multiply that hid it

Reading 32-bit was only half of it. Running it needs the loader to parse
Elf32 (16-byte symbols, info and value in different places, 4-byte address
fields), the machine to know its stack slot is four bytes wide, cdecl args
pushed right-to-left, and ret imm16 to clean up after itself.

Two decode holes surfaced once execution ran:

  mov eax, moffs (0xA0..0xA3) was missing entirely, so every stack-canary
  prologue left a gap and everything after it was read out of phase.

  one-operand imul/mul wrote only the low half and never touched edx. Every
  magic-number division a compiler emits reads its answer out of the high
  half, so division silently returned garbage or never terminated.

The second one was live at 64-bit too and had simply never been reached by a
case that divides. Both widths now agree with the native answer on the whole
corpus at every optimisation level: gcd, fib, fact, ack, sumsq, collatz,
clamp, popcnt, viadd, arr, sw, fptr, sumdigits, at O0 through Os.
