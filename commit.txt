Read 32-bit x86, and refuse the architectures we cannot

Two things a length decoder must never do: read one architecture's bytes as
another's, or do it silently. 7z.exe is a 32-bit i386 PE; the x86-64 decoder was
reading 0x41 (inc ecx) as a REX prefix and every byte after it was garbage, at a
confident 99% coverage.

  loader now reads the machine field of ELF, PE and Mach-O and carries the arch
  (x86-64, x86-32, arm64, ...). A binary that is not x86 is refused with its name
  rather than misdecoded.
  x86::decode_mode(bytes, addr, bits) decodes 32-bit as well as 64-bit: no REX,
  0x40..0x4F are the short inc/dec forms, mod=0 rm=5 is an absolute disp32 not
  RIP-relative, and ret imm16 / pushad / popad / pushfd / popfd are read.
  the audit routes by arch: recursive descent for x86-64, a mode-aware linear
  sweep for x86-32.

7z.exe now decodes in phase: 99% covered, zero ill-typed words (F=0), 859
functions whose control flow does not close. Execution (vox run) still needs the
64-bit machine; 32-bit audit, disasm, imasm and word are correct.
