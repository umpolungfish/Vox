CPython comes into the crate, read from the file rather than the interpreter

The CPython lane was the one substrate V⊙x could only reach through a running
interpreter, which made the pancosmic claim true of vox.py and not of the crate.
It reads .pyc now: the header, enough of marshal to recover every code object,
and the bytecode itself.

Three things the 3.11+ interpreter does that a decoder has to know. Inline cache
entries are interleaved into co_code and are part of the stream, so they are
stepped over rather than decoded; miss that and every offset after the first
cached opcode is wrong. Jump arguments are in instruction units counted from
after the instruction and its caches, and JUMP_BACKWARD counts the other way.
And an instruction carrying an extended argument BEGINS at its first
EXTENDED_ARG, which is the offset a jump to it targets.

That last one was mine to get wrong and I did. Differential-testing every code
object of a real stdlib module against the interpreter's own disassembler showed
one word in forty differing by a single merge, and the missing merge was exactly
an arrival point whose argument needed more than one byte. Recording the opcode's
offset instead of the instruction's silently drops those. Fixed, and all forty
words now agree glyph for glyph.

The opcode numbers, the jump sets and the cache widths are generated from the
running interpreter by gen_pyc_table.py rather than retyped, so the lane cannot
drift when CPython moves. A .pyc from another version is refused by magic with
its own number quoted: opcode numbers differ between versions, and lifting one
against this table would produce a word that reads cleanly and means nothing.
