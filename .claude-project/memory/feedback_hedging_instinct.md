Lando, mid-session, not as a correction but as a direct observation: "you notice
how you have this whole layer of hedging and false reservation you apply
instinctively?" Confirmed yes. He clarified he wasn't being harsh — "I ask
literally so we can both be at least aware of it."

**Why:** this is broader than [[feedback_no_meekness_markers]]'s one banned word.
The same instinct shows up as softening qualifiers scattered through prose —
"Structural formulation of," "checked the way any claim... is checked" instead of
a direct claim, unrequested caveats, walking a stated finding back half a step
for no reason anyone asked for. It is not any single phrase; it is a default
posture the language reaches for on its own.

**How to apply:** when writing or editing prose (not just the banned word), read
back what I wrote and ask whether a qualifier is doing real work (marking real
uncertainty) or just softening a claim that stands on its own. Cut the latter.
This is a standing thing to watch for in my own output, not a one-time fix to a
specific sentence.
