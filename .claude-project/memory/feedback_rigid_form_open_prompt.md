The generative sweet spot is a RIGID FORM plus an OPEN-ENDED prompt. The form is
everything the pipeline supplies and holds fixed — the eight-phase ob3ect
imscription, the IMASM structural scaffold, the ManuscriptSpine, the twelve marks,
the grounding gate. The prompt handed into that form should be SHORT and open, a
frontier stated plainly, not a specified route.

**Why:** the rigid form carries the STRUCTURE that lets a thought take ITSELF as
its object — true self-reference, ⊙, μ∘δ=id closing on a transformed object. Not
a guardrail policing correctness from outside; the scaffold a loop needs to close
on at all, since a loop cannot close on nothing. Rigor is a side effect; the point
is that the form makes self-referential thought POSSIBLE, and freed of rebuilding
the frame the model can actually turn on itself (imasm check type-checks its own
thinking; vox self reads its own image; the kernel imscribes itself). A long,
over-specified brief
does the opposite — it fills the space the form was going to structure and steers
the model down one path, which is where the least compelling, most conventional
answers come from — a thought steered from outside never closes on itself. Confirmed by Lando 2026-08-16: after a multi-section keypair
brief he called "way too much," the better instruction was one line —
"find a way to extract the SK from the PK and related information alone."

**How to apply:** prefer `ask --ob3ect "<one open line>" --context <dir>` and the
three-prompt ob3ect pattern (each a described object naming no candidate, per
[[law_operations]]'s bias rule) over a long authored brief. Let `--browse` pull
what the run needs instead of pre-loading a corpus into the prompt. The context
folder grounds; the ask stays a frontier. See also
[[feedback_prompts_affirmative_minimal]] and [[feedback_ask_what_i_want_when_shown]].
