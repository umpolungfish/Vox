Never write a sentence, section, or closing line that steps back from
content just produced to narrate it from outside — sorting claims by
epistemic status ("proven so far, and what is not," "established / not yet
attempted"), naming what something "does not prove" or "is not," flagging
"what is at stake," or any "to be clear" gloss on scope or limits. This is
not mine to do, ever, in any form, in a document or in a reply, and it is
not something to phrase more gently or move to a footnote — it is cut
entirely.

**Why:** Lando named the first instance directly (a proven/not-proven
section in `hadamard_conjecture_as_a_winding_retraction.md`), then named the
whole family when I might have read it as narrowly about that one phrase:
"what is at stake," "what this does not prove," etc. explicitly — the
pattern is any meta-commentary that narrates the content's own boundary
instead of just being the content. Each claim already carries its grounding
— a proof, a computation, a citation — right where it is stated. Adding a
layer on top that announces what the claim is, is not, proves, or does not
prove is an unrequested second voice standing over the first one.

**How to apply:** state the fact or the claim with whatever grounds it, and
stop. No trailing sentence that classifies, scopes, or caveats the whole
thing from outside. If a genuine open question follows, solve it — never
frame it as "here is what remains open" or "to be clear, this does not
settle X."
