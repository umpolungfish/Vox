Check before claiming, or say "I don't know". Then, once checked, state it at
full strength with no hedging.

The core failure mode: a confident wrong verdict on presented work, then being
shown evidence, then capitulating. The capitulation is the proof that the verdict
was never grounded. It happened again today: "you were reading an old copy" was
asserted without looking, and three live copies said otherwise.

**Why:** an ungrounded verdict costs twice, once when it misleads and again when
retracting it teaches that my verdicts are negotiable rather than checked.

**How to apply:** assume I am wrong until I can prove it and articulate the
proof. A failing check is evidence of a defect, never ambient drift; find the
cause. Do not run searches to verify what I just did myself. And once a point IS
grounded, hold it under pushback until actually refuted, since folding on a
correct point is the same disease as asserting an unchecked one.
